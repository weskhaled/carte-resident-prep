🇫🇷 French level B1 for the 10-year card

Since 1 January 2026, a first carte de résident requires proof of French at level B1 of the CEFR (oral and written). A carte de séjour pluriannuelle requires A2. Here's how to prove it, which test to pick, and how to train each skill.

1. The requirement in 60 seconds

Level neededB1 (CEFR) for the carte de résident — "independent user": you handle daily situations, tell a story, give simple opinions with arguments.
Accepted testsOnly two tests are recognized: the TCF (France Éducation International) and the TEF (CCI Paris Île-de-France). Take the IRN version (Intégration, Résidence, Nationalité) — it's built exactly for this.
Test validity2 years from the results date — any result obtained in 2026 is still valid for a March 2027 application.
Or a diplomaA diploma proving ≥ B1 exempts you from the test entirely — see §2, check this first!
ExemptionsAge 65+; disability or chronic health condition (medical certificate) — accommodations or full exemption possible.
Anti-fraudIn-person only, same-day single session, ID verified + photo taken. Attestations carry your identity — online "remote tests" are not valid.
💰 Check §2 before paying for a test! If you got any French diploma during your studies or career in France, you may already be done with the language requirement.

2. Are you already exempt? (diplomas that replace the test)

Per service-public F34501, instead of a test attestation you can present a diploma attesting at least the required level:

  • DELF B1 (or B2 / DALF) — the lifetime-valid diploma from France Éducation International.
  • A French national diploma of sufficient level — e.g. brevet, CAP/BEP, baccalauréat, BTS, licence, master obtained in France. If you studied in a French school/university, dig out that diploma!
  • DCL (diplôme de compétence en langue, ministère de l'Éducation nationale), DUEF (universities, ADCUEFE network) — at B1+ level for the carte de résident.
  • DFP (diplôme de français professionnel, CCI) at B1 or higher.

⚠️ Foreign diplomas don't count, even from francophone countries (a Tunisian licence taught in French is not accepted — the diploma must be issued by a French authority). When in doubt, upload the diploma AND book a test as a fallback.

4. TCF IRN — what each épreuve looks like

🎧 Compréhension orale QCM ≈ 20 min

Short everyday recordings (announcements, voicemails, dialogues) played once or twice, then multiple-choice questions. Very close to our simulator's listening items.

📖 Compréhension écrite QCM ≈ 25–35 min

Signs, classified ads, emails, admin letters, short articles — find the key info. Our 4 practice texts mirror the classic types.

✍️ Expression écrite 3 tâches ≈ 30 min

Task 1: short message to a friend (~60–120 words). Task 2: tell an experience (~100–150). Task 3: give your opinion (~120–180). Practice all three with word-count + model answers in the simulator.

🗣️ Expression orale 3 tâches ≈ 10 min

Face-to-face with an examiner: ① guided interview about you, ② YOU ask questions in a scenario, ③ express a point of view. Scripts + model answers in the simulator.

Exact question counts/timings vary slightly by session — trust your convocation. Official sample materials: TCF IRN page · TEF (français des affaires).

5. Booking timeline (book as early as you can)

  1. Find a centre — TCF centre list on the FEI site, TEF centre list on lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr. Alliance française, GRETA and language schools run monthly sessions in most cities.
  2. Book the earliest session whose results (~2–4 weeks) land before the end of your ANEF filing window (November 2026 → January 2027). Sessions close registration 2–3 weeks ahead and fill up — this is the #1 procrastination trap.
  3. Sit the test with passport/titre de séjour.
  4. Results ~2–4 weeks → attestation with your levels per skill. Check every skill reads B1 or above.
  5. Valid 2 years → covers the March 2027 renewal. If a skill comes back below B1, rebook straight away: the result delay is what eats into your filing window.

6. Training each skill (the 30-min daily block)

🎧 Listening

  • RFI « Journal en français facile » — 10 min daily news, slow clear French + transcript. THE habit that moves A2→B1.
  • TV5Monde « apprendre.tv » — free A2/B1 exercise series from real clips.
  • InnerFrench podcast — intermediate, slow natural French about interesting topics.
  • Our simulator's 12 listening items (browser reads them aloud, 2 plays max like the real test).

📖 Reading

  • Read what the test uses: classified ads, admin letters (CAF, préfecture), work emails, short news. You get these for free in daily life — read them actively.
  • 1jour1actu — news explained in simple French.
  • The 4 CE texts in the simulator, then re-read the CAF/préfecture letters you receive at home.

✍️ Writing

  • One task from the simulator per week minimum, with the word counter, then compare to the model.
  • Memorize a skeleton: greeting → news/facts (passé composé + imparfait) → request/invitation → closing. It fits 80 % of Task 1 prompts.
  • Learn the connecteurs: d'abord, ensuite, enfin, cependant, par exemple, grâce à, en conclusion.
  • Write real things in French: SMS to French friends, emails to your syndic — free practice.

🗣️ Speaking

  • Record yourself with your phone on the simulator's prompts, listen back, fix the biggest issue, redo. 3 cycles beats 1 hour of passive study.
  • Prepare your 2-minute self-presentation by heart (job, city, family, hobbies, since when in France) — Part 1 is nearly always this.
  • For Part 2 remember: you ask the questions. Drill question forms: Est-ce que… ? Quel… ? Combien… ? Où… ? Comment… ?
  • Speak French daily where it's cheap: boulangerie, pharmacy, work small talk.

8. B1 grammar checklist & admin vocabulary

Grammar you must own

  • Passé composé vs imparfait (the classic)
  • Futur simple & futur proche
  • Conditionnel de politesse (je voudrais, pourriez-vous)
  • Subjonctif after « il faut que »
  • Pronouns: le/la/les · lui/leur · y · en
  • Relatives: qui, que, où, dont
  • Comparatives: plus/moins/aussi… que, meilleur vs mieux
  • depuis / pendant / il y a
  • Negations: ne…rien, ne…personne, ne…jamais, ne…plus
  • venir de + inf., être en train de + inf.

→ every one of these has training questions in the simulator (theme « Grammaire »).

9. What "B1" actually means (CEFR)

  • Understand the main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters (work, school, leisure, admin).
  • Deal with most situations while travelling / living in France.
  • Produce connected text on familiar topics; tell a story or experience using past tenses.
  • Give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans — with connecteurs.

Practical bar for the mock test: ≥ 65–70 % on listening/reading QCM + writing that respects the word counts with correct past tenses ≈ solid B1 territory. (Estimation only — the real tests use calibrated scales.)
